# Five requests for a document

Tarthuth Veterinary Clinics is a fictional chain of veterinary clinics. Its two-person data team
built the request urgency tagger, a small Python project that sorts owners' appointment messages
into urgent, soon and routine. Each request below comes from one reader, named by role. Decide
what that reader must do with the document, then outline it.

## Scenario 1

A new developer joins the data team next week. On the first morning they must get the tagger
running on their own laptop, tag the sample messages and prove that their copy works before they
change anything. Nobody will be free to sit with them.

## Scenario 2

The lead veterinarian has been asked to approve a change before any code is written: the tagger
would read the animal's species from the message and send every livestock message to a
veterinarian's queue. She wants to see how the change would work, what it touches and what could
go wrong, so that she can approve it, ask for changes or refuse it.

## Scenario 3

The operations director must choose which tagger the reception desk uses next quarter: the keyword
rules, Model S or Model L. The data team has measured all three. He has ten minutes for the
question and will sign one option.

## Scenario 4

The data team lead ran a second evaluation with a new prompt. She needs a record of what was run,
on which data, what came out, how sure the team can be, and what to try next, so that the result
can be repeated and compared with the next run.

## Scenario 5

The head of reception will decide how far her staff may rely on the tag: which messages they can
leave in the queue and which they must read at once. She does not read code. She needs to know
what the tagger is for, what it is not for, how well it does on which kinds of message, and where
it fails.
