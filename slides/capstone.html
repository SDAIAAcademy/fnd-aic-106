Documentation set review

Capstone: Each participant shows the set in four minutes, then answers four minutes of questions on its claims and its history

The follow-up review, in groups of six, one hour per group. Before the group arrives, open each participant's repository link once, so that nobody discovers a private repository at the front. The review is theirs: keep your own talking to the opening and the close.

How the review runs

Openingthe rubric, the running order, the history check

Sixty minutes: opening 3, six participants each with 4 minutes of showing, 4 of questions and the history check, and 1 of changeover (54), close 3. A group of fewer than six finishes early; never shorten a participant's minutes to fit a seventh.

The task

One project, four documents, each for its own reader

README

A stranger installs, runs and checks it on a clean machine.

Decision memo

One decision: options, trade-offs, recommendation, what would change it.

Model or system card

Intended use, slices with intervals, limits with actions, three roles.

Revision log

Every assisted change checked, and a disclosure line.

No project of your own? Document the urgency tagger at version 0.4, from the second evaluation log in labs/files/capstone/.

The brief is labs/files/capstone/brief.md. Participants on the tagger decide whether reception should move from version 0.3 to 0.4; the lab documents are first drafts and must be revised for the new run, not copied. Own projects use only public or synthetic data.

How a set is scored

The rubric

Card: intended use, slices with uncertainty, known limits
25%
README: a stranger installs, runs and checks it
20%
Memo: options, trade-offs, recommendation, conditions
20%
Revision log: each change checked, a disclosure line
20%
Written for the named reader, every document
15%

Each criterion scores one document, and the last scores the writing across all four, so nothing is counted twice: a missing interval costs marks on the card, a hedge without a number costs marks under the last line. Walk through what full marks look like for each line.

Authenticity: the memo was written in session; revision logs and document history are inspected, which is the next slide.

Pick one flagged row and one unflagged row. A participant who cannot show the evidence a row names loses the marks of the revision-log line, whatever the document looks like. A set uploaded in one commit is not evidence of drafting; ask how it was written.

Submission

One public GitHub repository link per participant

  • The project, or a link to it, and its README at the top.
  • The memo, the card and the revision log with its disclosure line.
  • The commit history of the drafts.

Individual

Only public or synthetic material; only Public text into any assistant.

Remind participants to make the repository public before the review, not during it. Nothing personal, classified or proprietary goes into it.
